From “I feel” to “I see.”

A shared record helps teachers and coaches examine the same classroom moment.

What is micro-teaching?

Teach and record a short segment, examine it against a goal, discuss evidence, test a small adjustment, and review the results. Ask who answered, how much thinking time was available, and what students actually demonstrated.

Engagement impressions alone do not establish understanding. Video helps us revisit instructional decisions with curiosity.

Empower

Teacher chooses a meaningful instructional goal. What do you want to learn about your teaching?

Implement

Record a focused segment. What instructional moment should we examine?

Investigate

Review the evidence together. What patterns do we notice?

Realize

Identify growth and next steps. What might you try next, and how will you review the results?

Trust comes before the camera

  • Teacher participation, voice, and ownership
  • Agree on a narrow goal and who may view the recording
  • Follow district recording, consent, access, storage, and deletion policies
  • Separate coaching from formal evaluation wherever district policy permits
  • Make only confidentiality commitments that policy can support
  • Let the teacher review privately before a shared conversation

Professional learning that makes a difference

Dr. Glenda Horner’s central advice: keep the session focused. Experience one observation and practice one protocol.

Know your learners

Ask about coaching responsibilities, concerns, and what would be useful.

Decide what matters most

Use classroom evidence to support one reflective coaching conversation.

Create connections

Connect prior experiences through short, structured nearby conversations.

Show and practice

Model Notice-Wonder-Next, then let colleagues try it.

Build in reflection

Make room for quiet observation and thinking before discussion.

Plan for changed practice

Choose a specific action and a date to revisit it.
