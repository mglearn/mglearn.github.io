Watch → think → compare

  1. Set the question (1 minute).
  2. Watch the selected excerpt (about 2 minutes).
  3. Record two observations silently (1 minute).
  4. Compare with a nearby partner (2 minutes).
  5. Debrief evidence versus interpretation (3 minutes).

Observe: teacher questions, pauses, student responses, and follow-up questions.

Evidence: “The teacher paused before inviting a response.” Interpretation: “Students were more engaged because the teacher waited.” The latter requires additional evidence.

Examine instructional decisions with curiosity. This is practice observing evidence, not grading the recorded teacher.

Video unavailable? Use this fictional classroom case.

A middle school science teacher asks a question and calls on the first raised hand. The student answers correctly. The same student answers the next question. Later the teacher asks whether everyone understands; several students nod. The teacher moves on.

Notice

We heard answers from one student, not responses from everyone.

Wonder

How could the teacher find out whether the other students understood?

Next

Ask every student to write a response before inviting individual answers.

This fictional stimulus preserves conversation practice when video fails. It does not provide the evidence available in an actual recording.
