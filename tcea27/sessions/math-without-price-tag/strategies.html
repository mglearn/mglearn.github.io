The strategy behind each experience

Connect the demonstration to its instructional purpose. Open the context notes before interpreting any research value.

Read the evidence in context.

These are strategy-level research findings, not effect sizes for GraphSplat, Digital Math Tools, or Quiz and Flashcard Studio. Values below are the Weighted mean effect size in each current MetaX record, not its separate table average. They summarize varied studies, not a guaranteed gain. No effect sizes are added together.

Teacher clarity

Make the learning goal and evidence of success understandable before the task.

Try it: Before comparing graphs, say: “Use the axis scale and the values to explain how the same counts can look different.”

Direct instruction

Model a process, check understanding, then gradually give learners more responsibility.

Try it: Think aloud as you count three jumps from 4 on a number line. Ask learners to solve a similar example and explain the jumps.

Corrective feedback

Point to a specific gap, give a usable cue, and allow a revised response.

Try it: If a learner says Garden B has twice as many insects, cue them to read the labels and calculate 15 minus 12.

Retrieval practice

Recall an idea before looking back, then check the explanation and try again later.

Transfer strategies

Recognize when an established idea helps with a different problem and adapt it deliberately.

Try it: Apply the same scale-checking routine to a campus temperature graph and explain what changes for a line graph.
