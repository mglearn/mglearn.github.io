Lesson resources

Turn the experience into a lesson.

Adapt a starting point to your learners, then verify the full standard and your district sequence. These short examples support selected expectations; they are not a complete curriculum.

K–2

Count the jumps, not the starting point

Learning intention: Explain joining and separating with objects, drawings, and a number line.

Try it: Model 4 + 3 with counters, then show three one-unit jumps from 4 to 7. Ask what each jump represents.

Listen for: Learners distinguish the starting point from the three moves and explain the sum.

Feedback & extension: Cue: “Show each space you traveled.” Extend to a new start and compare methods.

TEKS connection: Kindergarten K.3(A)–(C): model joining/separating, solve within 10, and explain strategies. The number line is an instructional bridge; begin with the objects and drawings named in the standard.

3–5

Garden evidence and fair comparisons

Learning intention: Represent category counts and justify a comparison using a scaled bar graph.

Try it: Use the synthetic counts 12 and 15. Build a zero-baseline bar graph, explain the 3-insect difference, and critique a second display starting at 10.

Listen for: Learners use the axis, values, and a calculation as evidence rather than relying only on apparent bar heights.

Feedback & extension: Cue: “Where do the bars start?” Extend by collecting equal-effort samples with additional categories.

TEKS connection: Grade 3: 3.8(A)–(B), summarize categorical data using displays including scaled bar graphs and solve one- and two-step problems from them.

6–8

Compare groups before making a claim

Learning intention: Represent and compare numerical distributions, not just isolated category totals.

Try it: Collect repeated equal-effort insect counts in two locations. Use dot plots or box plots; compare center, spread, overlap, and sampling limitations.

Listen for: Learners support a comparison using the distribution and acknowledge that a single count is insufficient.

Feedback & extension: Cue: “What do repeated samples show?” Extend to a second day and discuss variation.

TEKS connection: Grade 6: 6.12(A), represent numerical data with displays including dot plots and box plots; 6.13(A), interpret those summaries. The two-garden bar example is the launch, not a full match to these distribution expectations.

TEA Chapter 111 source ↗ · Verified 2026-10-27

9–12

Relationships are not causes

Learning intention: Fit a linear relationship to paired data and distinguish prediction from a causal claim.

Try it: Measure sunlight hours and plant height for several plants. Make a scatter plot, consider a reasonable linear model, and discuss variables not controlled.

Listen for: Learners interpret a model, make a supported prediction, and explain why association alone does not show sunlight caused a specific change.

Feedback & extension: Cue: “What else could differ?” Extend to a new sample and test the model’s limitations.

TEKS connection: Algebra I: A.4(A)–(C), interpret correlation, distinguish association and causation, and fit linear functions to data for predictions.
