Project Management

Beyond the Rollout: Project Management That Works in K–12

Presenter-led models and brief decision discussions for K–12 technology leaders. No preparation or project required.

Move district initiatives beyond the rollout.

Successful technology initiatives require more than technical expertise. They demand clear planning, stakeholder buy-in, effective communication, and the ability to lead change. In this 50-minute presenter-led session, K–12 technology leaders examine practical strategies for device refreshes, cybersecurity rollouts, infrastructure upgrades, and AI implementation. Follow a shared district scenario through worked examples of project scope, stakeholders, milestones, risks, communication, and key performance indicators (KPIs). Short seated discussions invite you to choose a next action, examine resistance, and check evidence of adoption. Leave with a practical framework, reusable planning templates, and actionable strategies to move district technology initiatives from good ideas to measurable results. No preparation or project required.

Follow the shared caseOpen presenter guide

Learning objectives

  • Define scope and identify stakeholders, decision authority, milestones, and dependencies in a district technology initiative.
  • Choose risk responses and communication that build stakeholder buy-in.
  • Distinguish delivery KPIs from indicators of use and sustained adoption.
  • Use a worked example to identify a next action and a response to resistance.

Success criteria

  • Identify a clear outcome, an exclusion, and an accountable milestone owner in the shared case.
  • Explain a risk response and a stakeholder communication choice.
  • Describe one delivery KPI and one adoption KPI with an evidence source and review point.
  • Recommend a next action that addresses a barrier and supports sustained use.

A shared case, taught step by step.

A fictional portable classroom remains offline after work stalled between facilities and IT. The principal receives assurances but no dependable completion date. Use only fictional or generalized details.

Follow presenter models, think briefly, discuss a decision with a neighbor, and compare with the worked example. No project, preparation, or device required.
