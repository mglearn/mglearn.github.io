Participate

Let’s play, explore, and explain together.

Open the game, follow the group routine, and keep a classroom idea you can use.

0–9 minutes

Let’s start with a game

Your concept Bingo card

Choose a clue with the group, then mark the matching concept. Before claiming a line, explain the matches. Winning is optional; explaining is the point.

FREE
Explain a choice

Choose Next clue when you’re ready.

All clues and explanations · also works on paper
  1. You close your notes and try to recall the idea before looking back. Retrieval practice
  2. A classmate explains why the answer makes sense, not just which option to click. Peer explanation
  3. You point to a detail, example, or result that supports your claim. Evidence
  4. A useful cue helps you correct a misconception and try again. Feedback
  5. You describe how your thinking changed after the activity. Reflection
  6. Your group works toward one product that needs everyone’s contribution. Shared goal
  7. You use what learners say or do to decide what teaching comes next. Formative assessment
  8. You connect a new idea to an example and explain how they relate. Elaboration

Cards and marks are independent on each device; nothing is sent to a live room. Bingo Caller can project a custom call list. Its room name does not synchronize separate attendee devices without an added relay.

9–18 minutes

What makes the learning cooperative?

Navigator

Open the activity and invite others to direct the next action.

Explainer

Say why an answer or choice makes sense.

Questioner

Ask for evidence or an example; make space for another voice.

Connector

Record the group’s idea and connect it to a classroom goal.

Work in groups of three or four. With three, combine Navigator and Connector. Swap roles in round two; a role never stops anyone else from thinking or explaining.

Try → explain → question → revise

Before clicking, give each person thinking time. After a choice, ask “Why does that fit?” Invite someone else to extend or question the explanation, then revise together.

18–34 minutes

Try two activities together

Review and recall

Round one: Bingo. Round two: Quiz and Flashcard Studio.

Ask everyone to retrieve before comparing answers.

Puzzles and reasoning

Round one: Meme Puzzle. Round two: Fortune Wheel.

After a reveal or solved phrase, ask for evidence and an example.

Create and explain

Round one: Story Wheel. Round two: Dicebreaker Creator.

Explain a sequence, then use a follow-up to extend an idea.

34–42 minutes

Show us what you discovered

  1. Use one minute each to explain to a nearby group: “We tried ____. Learners would explain ____. We’d look for ____.”
  2. Ask one follow-up about the evidence or participation.
  3. We’ll hear up to three short examples from the room.

You don’t need to move seats or share anyone’s personal information. Speak, write, or ask a partner to share your contribution.

47–50 minutes

Check what you can now explain

Which change makes the game more cooperative?

Check explanation

A common outcome plus each person’s contribution gives the group a reason to listen and explain. Points alone do not structure cooperative learning.

When does the quiz invite retrieval practice?

Check explanation

Try recalling before looking. Then check the explanation, correct errors, and revisit the idea later.

What makes the check formative?

Check explanation

Use evidence of learner thinking to adjust instruction, provide a specific cue, and invite another attempt. Completion alone is not enough.
