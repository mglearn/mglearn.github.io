Cooperative learning

Play, Explore, Explain: Cooperative Learning in Action

Let’s play, listen to one another, and make the thinking visible.

What happens after the game?

Want students to do more than play a game or complete a digital activity? Let’s try the activities together and look closely at the thinking they invite. We’ll begin with a short learning game, then work in small groups to explore review, problem-solving, creativity, and formative assessment. You’ll try a shared task, explain your choices, listen to a colleague, and consider what you’d adapt for your learners. Leave with practical ideas and reusable activities. Bring a smartphone, tablet, or laptop; one shared device per group works too.

Bring: Smartphone, tablet, or laptop; one shared device per small group is enough.

Learning outcomes

  • Experience cooperative learning activities that encourage participation, explanation, and reflection.
  • Explore instructional activities that support retrieval practice, formative assessment, and deeper learning.
  • Identify and share practical ways to adapt interactive activities for classroom use.

Success criteria

  • Help your group complete a shared task while every person explains a choice.
  • Describe evidence of retrieval, peer explanation, or formative assessment in an activity.
  • Adapt an activity with a learning goal, a participation routine, and a way to check understanding.

Our fifty-minute plan

  1. 0–9 min
    Play a short concept Bingo game

    Think about a clue, mark a concept, and explain why it fits.

  2. 9–18 min
    Notice what makes the learning cooperative

    Choose a shared goal, give everyone thinking time, and agree how you’ll take turns.

  3. 18–34 min
    Explore two activities with your group

    Try one tool in each eight-minute round. Rotate roles and record what learners would explain.

  4. 34–42 min
    Share what you discovered

    Offer a short example, a piece of learning evidence, and one question.

  5. 42–47 min
    Make one activity work for your classroom

    Choose a learning goal, a prompt, a participation routine, and a check for understanding.

  6. 47–50 min
    Explain what you’ll take with you

    Answer a short retrieval check and name one adjustment you’ll try.

A shared task needs every voice.

We’ll give everyone quiet thinking time, a chance to explain, and a reason to listen. The goal is a shared explanation you can all give—not a race to let one person finish.

You can share a device, speak, write, or ask a partner to read your contribution. Printed cards and prompts are in the toolkit if connectivity is unreliable.

Workshop photos are AI-generated illustrations, not photographs of actual attendees or a documented event.
